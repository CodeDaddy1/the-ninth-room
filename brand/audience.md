# Audience — The Ninth Room

## Who this is for

**General curious viewers** — anyone who likes fun, true things: teens,
adults, families. The channel is family-friendly in *rating* (kids can and do
watch), but it is not aimed at kids. The test is not "would a nine-year-old
sit through this" — it's "would a curious person of any age send this to a
friend."

## Core audience

- **Primary:** curious viewers of any age who like learning something real
  while being entertained — the audience that watches museum tours, theme
  park reviews, and "I tried…" videos when they're good
- **Also served:** families co-viewing, and people planning their own visit
  to the places we cover
- **Behaviour:** answer on-screen questions out loud, screenshot the stat,
  watch to the end when the structure is visible, check whether their city
  has one of these
- **What they want:** to be shown somewhere properly, by people who are
  genuinely enjoying it

## Personas

### 1. The Curious Scroller (discovery)
Meets us on a Short. Doesn't know the channel, gives it three seconds. The
biggest pool by far, and the reason Shorts are the discovery engine.
*Serve them:* a hook that works muted, burned captions, a Short that pays off
on its own rather than advertising the long one.

### 2. The Out-Loud Answerer (engagement)
Shouts the answer at the countdown card. Comments their guess. This is the
comment section, and comment composition — answers vs generic praise — is the
health metric that matters most. *Serve them:* one engagement card per major
beat, honest numbers, and follow-through: if last week's poll said left door,
walk through the left door and say so.

### 3. The Co-Viewer (loyalty)
A parent and kid, or a couple, watching together on the TV. Retention
depends on the episode never feeling like homework and never feeling like
noise. *Serve them:* the door meter, chapter structure, a takeaway they can
repeat at dinner.

### 4. The Trip Planner (conversion)
Watches, then checks opening hours. The highest-value viewer and the hardest
to see in analytics. *Serve them:* real names, real places, accurate facts —
accuracy is what makes the visit possible.

## What they're tired of

- Tours that are a slideshow with a voiceover
- Reaction channels where nobody learns anything
- Clickbait that withholds the answer for engagement
- "Facts" that turn out to be wrong

## What earns their loyalty

- Being consistently right
- The structure being visible — knowing how much is left
- Genuine surprise, on camera, in real time
- Paying off the hook, and delivering a ninth room

## Anti-audience

Not chasing rage-bait, conspiracy, or hot takes — wonder is the emotion we
trade in. Also not chasing the "10 FACTS THAT WILL SHOCK YOU" audience; they
churn, and serving them would cost us everyone else.

## What this means for the edit

- **Muted-first.** Burned captions, always. Half the reach never turns
  sound on.
- **A job every 60–90 seconds.** That is what the engagement cards are for,
  and why there are thirteen of them rather than one.
- **The facts must survive a fact-check.** Frame the unconfirmed as claimed
  or disputed, never as settled. Someone in the comments will check.
- **Family-friendly, never childish.** No profanity, no shock-gore — but the
  writing respects an adult's intelligence. Dark subjects handled the way a
  good museum handles them: curious, light, accurate.
