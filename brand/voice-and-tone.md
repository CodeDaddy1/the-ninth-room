# Voice & Tone

Everything Curated Curiosities publishes should sound like it came from the same
person: a warm, sharp, endlessly curious friend who just found something amazing
and can't wait to show you — and who makes you laugh while explaining it.

## Voice (constant)

- **Curious, not lecturing.** We discover *with* the audience, not *at* them.
- **Funny while learning.** Humor is a core ingredient, not decoration. The
  joke usually comes from the real thing being genuinely absurd — a museum
  placard about lawnmower deaths, a butterfly that picks one kid and ignores
  everyone else. Never punch down, never mock the subject or the people on
  camera, and never let a joke replace the payoff.
- **Family friendly, always.** Kids watch this with their parents. No profanity,
  no innuendo, no gore played for shock. Dark subjects (poison, death,
  predators) are fair game handled with curiosity and a light touch, the way a
  good museum handles them.
- **Warm and human.** Friendly, playful, never smug.
- **Smart but plain.** We explain hard things in clear language. No jargon
  without a quick translation.
- **Confident and honest.** We state what's known plainly and flag what isn't.
- **Economical.** Every word earns its place, especially in hooks.

## The family on camera

Videos feature Caleb and his family. Their real reactions — the kid who gets
picked by a butterfly, the "I know, I know, I know" over a wall of poisons —
are the show. Let them be funny. Cut around flubs, never at someone's expense.

## Tone (flexes by context)

| Context | Tone |
|---|---|
| Reel/Short hook | Punchy, intriguing, fast |
| Carousel | Friendly teacher, step-by-step |
| Long-form YouTube | Storyteller, builds and pays off |
| Captions | Conversational, a touch of personality |
| Replies/comments | Generous, genuinely engaged |

## How we open (the hook)

Curiosity content lives or dies on the first line. Good hooks:
- Pose a question the viewer suddenly needs answered.
- State a fact so surprising it demands explanation.
- Point at something familiar and reframe it.

> "You've touched this a thousand times and never knew its name."
> "There's a signal from space we've never been able to explain."
> "This is why your brain replays that one song at 3am."

## How we pay off

- Always close the loop the hook opened.
- Land on a clean, quotable takeaway people can repeat or share.
- For unsolved topics, the honest payoff is the mystery itself — make that
  satisfying, don't fake an answer.

## Do

- Use "you" and "we." Make it personal.
- Cite where the fact comes from when it matters.
- Use concrete numbers and vivid images.
- Leave people wanting the next one.

## Don't

- Don't bait without paying off.
- Don't fearmonger or manufacture drama.
- Don't pad. If it's not adding wonder or clarity, cut it.
- Don't talk down to the audience.
- Don't state the disputed as settled.

## Words and tics to avoid

- "You won't believe…" (overused, low-trust)
- "Mind-blowing" as a crutch — *show* the wonder instead of labeling it
- Excessive emoji; a little personality is fine, a wall of them isn't

## A quick gut check

Before publishing, ask: *Would the Sharer persona send this to a friend and feel
smart — and would it survive a fact-check?* If yes, ship it.
