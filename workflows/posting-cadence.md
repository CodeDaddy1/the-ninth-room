# Release Rhythm — The Ninth Room

**An episode ships when it's ready.** There is no calendar promise — shoots
are real family outings and the edit takes what it takes. What keeps the
channel present between episodes is Shorts. This doc describes the rhythm of
the work, not a schedule.

## The two outputs

| | What | When |
|---|---|---|
| **Episode** | One full visit, chaptered, 4K landscape — the product | When the edit is right |
| **Shorts** | Standalone vertical cuts from finished episodes — the discovery engine | Steadily between episodes |

## Why no calendar

A promised weekly slot forces one of two failures: shipping an episode that
isn't ready, or burning the family out to feed the queue. Both cost more
audience than an irregular cadence does. Growth comes from every episode
being genuinely good and from Shorts keeping the channel discoverable in the
gaps — not from hitting a day of the week.

What IS consistent: the shape of every episode (hook → chapters on the door
meter → ninth-room moment → takeaway → outro), the voice, and the promise
that the payoff lands.

## The cycle per episode

1. **Scout** — pick the place; check it has ninth-room potential.
2. **Shoot** — the visit. Shoot for the cards: a `countdown` needs a reveal
   to cut to, a `vote` needs the family actually disagreeing on camera.
   Bank b-roll generously; it's free while you're there.
3. **Post** — story loop → edit plan → graphics + captions → assemble →
   review → grade → master (see `content-workflow.md`).
4. **Ship** — episode, then thumbnail/title packaging pass.
5. **Cut Shorts** — mine the finished episode for standalone moments; release
   them steadily until the next episode lands.
6. **Listen** — comments shape the next scout. A `this_that` vote asked in
   one episode is **honoured in the next** — real numbers on a `poll` card,
   and then actually do what the audience picked.

## Shorts strategy (the discovery engine)

A Short is not a trailer. It pays off on its own, in its own 30–60 seconds,
and earns the click on merit.

- The best self-contained moments from the episode, recut vertical — not the
  episode compressed.
- One engagement card maximum, answerable in the first five seconds
  (`countdown` and `this_that` are the two that survive the length).
- Burned captions always; most Shorts views are muted.
- Vertical insets: 180 top, 320 bottom, 64 sides. The renderer handles this;
  don't nudge it.
- Release them spread out, not dumped — they are the channel's presence
  between episodes.

## Follow-through (non-negotiable)

Asking for a vote and ignoring it teaches the audience the cards are
decoration. Every question asked on screen gets its answer in a later
episode. That is the single cheapest loyalty mechanism the channel has.

## Sustainability

- Better a great episode every few weeks forever than weekly for two months.
- If time is short, protect the episode; drop Shorts output first.
- Bank footage on every visit — a place already shot is a buffer.
