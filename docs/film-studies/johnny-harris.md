# Film study: Johnny Harris — "The Worst War You Never Learned About, Mapped"

- **Source:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=czQrU0OPIR8 (channel: Johnny
  Harris, ~7.9M subs). Published 2023-03-15.
- **Runtime:** 16:26 (985.6s measured), 10,844,758 views, 220,683 likes
  (2.03% like-to-view — top decile for the format).
- **Why this one:** picked by evidence, not taste. Pulled the channel's
  full upload list with yt-dlp and sorted by views; this is the
  **highest-viewed Johnny Harris video inside the 8–20 minute band**
  (#3 on the channel overall behind two ~28-minute pieces), and it is from
  the "Mapped" line — the map-driven storytelling this study was
  commissioned to dissect. The video also contains an on-camera statement
  of intent at 15:44: *"I hope you enjoyed this map video… what complex
  issues would benefit from being mapped in this way."*
- **Studied:** 2026-08-24, four passes — 100 scene-aware frames full-range
  (512px), 57 frames over 0:00–1:00 at 1024px, 45 targeted cue frames at
  the silences / title card / name reveals, and eight contact sheets of the
  lower-third band sampled every 4s to census the date strip. **95 of the
  100 full-range frames were read**; the 5 unread all fall inside the
  Nebula outro (14:50–15:40).
- **Transcript:** YouTube auto-captions (word-timed, lowercase, no
  punctuation). Speech is clean studio VO so the words are reliable;
  proper-noun casing is not ("bosniaks", "milosevic", "serps"). All quotes
  below are re-cased by me, never re-worded.
- **Cut measurement:** ffmpeg scene detection at two thresholds (0.30 =
  118 cuts, 0.15 = 160 cuts) so the numbers are directly comparable to the
  two Hangtime studies, which used 0.30. **The caveat matters more here
  than it did there:** this video's primary device is a map that changes
  state continuously without cutting. Scene detection cannot see a morph,
  a fly-through or a crossfade, so these counts are *hard cuts only* — and
  that under-count is itself the headline finding, not an error bar.
- **Genre distance:** a studio-funded eight-person geopolitics documentary
  (credits at 13:40 list animator, editor, senior producer, EP, composer,
  production manager) about a genocide, versus a family exploring a museum.
  Nothing about the subject transfers. What transfers is the **grammar of
  a persistent graphic that carries the story while footage plays over
  it** — which is exactly the problem our episodes have when a room has
  more to explain than a face can carry.

## 2. The rules

Each rule is stated so a person could follow it tomorrow, with the
timestamp that proves it and the number that makes it checkable.

### Hook anatomy

**R1 — The title carries the curiosity gap. The hook carries momentum.**
The first words are the story's first event, in the historical present, at
a dated moment. No question, no restatement of the title, no "in this
video", no self-reference of any kind before 2:48.
> Evidence: **0:00** — *"It's 1991 and the Soviet Union is fracturing."*
> 39 words in the first 15.5 seconds. The words "Bosnian War" — the
> subject of the video — are not spoken until **2:37.7**, 16% of the way
> in.

**R2 — The hook is the slowest-cut section, not the fastest.**
Cold open = **4.1 cuts/min**. The sponsor read = **12.4 cuts/min**. The
outro = 10.5. The opening is cut at one third the rate of the ad inside it.
> Evidence: first hard cut at **0:14.93**. The entire first 15 seconds is
> one unbroken shot.

**R3 — Four shots in the first 72 seconds.**
Map 14.9s → archival 1.5s → archival 1.5s → map 54.6s. That is the whole
cold-open cutting plan.
> Evidence: cuts at 14.93, 16.48, 17.98, then nothing until **72.53**.

**R4 — Inside a long graphic shot, state changes replace cuts — one change
every ~3 seconds.**
Inside the 54.6s map shot the board changes state at least ten times and
never cuts.
> Evidence: **0:21** strip flips to `INDEPENDENCE WARS` · **0:24** all
> republics but three fade to white · **0:27** camera flies to Macedonia
> alone, badge gets a green dot · **0:30** camera pulls to Slovenia +
> Croatia, red dots appear along the border · **0:33** Serbia fills red ·
> **0:43** Montenegro joins.

**R5 — The title card is punctuation on a music sting, not on the spoken
phrase, and it arrives after the setup is finished.**
It lands at **2:45.5**, holds ~3 seconds, and reads only `BOSNIAN WAR` —
white caps in a black bar over a blown-out, defocused archival frame with a
giant ghosted duplicate of the same two words behind it. He said "the
Bosnian War" **8 seconds earlier** at 2:37.7; the card waits for the sting
at 2:41 to resolve.

### Narrative foretelling

**R6 — Every proper noun the VO introduces becomes a badge that stays on
screen until its role in the story ends.**
The board accumulates: 1 badge at 0:00, 6 by 0:12, 3 by 3:45, 7 by 7:48.
Nothing is introduced twice, and nothing you were told about disappears
while it still matters.
> Evidence: **3:45** — Yugoslav People's Army, SR Serbia, Milosevic stack
> as a right-hand column while Republika Srpska, HVO/Herzeg-Bosnia and
> Bosniaks hold the left. **7:48** — UN and NATO have joined the left; 7
> badges co-resident.

**R7 — The frame's left/right encodes the sides, and the badges carry a
one-pixel state dot.**
Anti-Serb actors park left, Serb actors right, for the whole video. A
**green dot** on a badge means peaceful or allied; **red dots on the map**
mean fighting.
> Evidence: **0:27** Macedonia's badge gets a green dot on *"Macedonia is
> allowed to leave peacefully"*. **0:30** Slovenia and Croatia get red
> dots strung along their borders on *"Slovenia and Croatia fight wars"*.
> **9:10–9:36** HVO and Bosniaks both carry green dots while re-allied.

**R8 — The chapter marker never interrupts. It is a bar, not a break.**
`YEAR | MONTH ▶ EVENT` — gold caps, red play triangle, year and month on a
slot-machine odometer that rolls to the new date. 21 events, one every
~34s of story, and the video never once cuts to a full-screen chapter
title after 2:45.
> Evidence: **0:03** `1991 | JAN ▶ FALL OF SOVIET UNION` with 1990/1992
> visibly ghosted above and below the year mid-roll.

**R9 — The section header leads the voice; the name card trails it by
1.5s.**
The strip flips **0.4–0.9s before** the spoken noun. A person's name card
starts building **within ±0.5s** of the name and finishes resolving
**~1.5s after**, via a character-scramble.
> Evidence (strip): at **7.5s** the strip still reads `FALL OF SOVIET
> UNION`; by **8.0s** it reads `YUGOSLAVIA IS FALLING APART`; the word
> "Yugoslavia" is spoken at **~8.4s**.
> Evidence (name): *"his name was Slobodan Milosevic"* runs 2:29.3–2:31.
> The portrait badge starts materialising at **2:30.5**, reads
> `SLO D M V C` mid-scramble at **2:31.0**, and resolves to
> `SLOBODAN MILOSEVIC` at **2:32.0**.

### Map-driven storytelling

**R10 — The graphic is the spine and it never cuts away. Footage is
composited ON it, not instead of it.**
Archival plays inside a soft circular porthole with a distressed edge —
and the edge is *transparent*, so the map keeps running underneath and
around it. This is why the video can hold 40–60s shots without feeling
static: the "cut" to footage is a layer, not a cut.
> Evidence: **0:20** the burning-Dubrovnik porthole has the Serbia and
> Macedonia badges clearly visible through its edge. **13:40** the credits
> arrive as a dark left panel while the map keeps animating on the right
> 55% of frame — the graphic outlives the story.

**R11 — Never name the place. Point at it and move the camera there.**
18 deictic phrases in 2,779 words, and the camera flight begins *with or
before* the phrase, not after.
> Evidence: **0:05** *"and down here another Communist party is failing"*
> — the camera is already flying south out of the post-Soviet map; by
> **0:08** it has arrived over Yugoslavia and the strip has already
> changed. He never says "in the Balkans."

**R12 — A counted noun gets counted pins.**
> Evidence: *"Yugoslavia, a Federation of **six** countries"* (0:08–0:10)
> → **0:12–0:21**, six numbered black circles 1–6, each tethered by a
> hairline to its own flag badge. The number is not written as a stat; it
> is instantiated as objects you can count.

**R13 — When you name somewhere the viewer knows, fly there at the same
map scale rather than cutting to stock.**
> Evidence: **7:20** and **11:01**, both on *"the United States"* — the
> map flies to a blue-filled USA on the identical canvas, badges still
> parked at the frame edge, then flies back. Two occurrences, both times
> the country is named, never for decoration.

### The peak

**R14 — At the emotional peak, stop narrating. Twice, for 19s and 13s.**
> Evidence: **9:21–9:40** — 19 seconds with no VO between *"take Sarajevo
> with overwhelming force"* and *"…a UN safe zone where nearly 10,000
> Bosniaks are seeking refuge"*, filled with archival and one strip
> change. **10:25–10:38** — 13 seconds of no VO immediately after *"the
> deadliest genocide on European soil since World War II"*.

**R15 — The silence is where the primary source speaks, subtitled, with
its receipts underneath.**
Two-tier card: the quote in bold white sentence case, and beneath it a
small grey line naming who said it and how we know what happened to them.
> Evidence: **10:32** *"Don't be afraid. Slowly, slowly, let the women and
> children go first."* / **10:37** *"Don't be afraid. Nobody will harm
> you."* — both carrying `Ratko Mladic, "The Butcher of Bosnia." A Serb
> general who was imprisoned for life by the ICC for his war crimes.`
> The attribution is not a citation dump; it is the dramatic irony, set in
> type.

**R16 — The payoff is the opening canvas with every label stripped off.**
> Evidence: **13:08–13:31** — the same map, now three flat colours, **zero
> badges, zero strip, zero labels**, held for 22.9s. Everything the video
> spent 13 minutes teaching you to read, and it says nothing. The board is
> cleared because you no longer need it.

### Tense and voice

**R17 — Three tenses, switched on purpose.**
Historical present for the whole story; a hard switch to simple past the
moment the outcome is known; true present for "today".
> Evidence: *"is fracturing"* (0:01), *"he recognizes"* (2:32), *"Serbs
> escalate"* (5:08) → **12:38** *"so the war is over, around a hundred
> thousand people **died**… Milosevic **went** to jail… he **died** before
> the end of his trial"* → **13:10** *"**today** Bosniaks, Serbs and Croats
> live in relative peace."*

**R18 — 163 wpm, and the narrator is not a character.**
One first-person line in 13:40 of story (**5:27**). The "I" and "we"
appear only inside the two ad blocks, where he becomes a person again.

### Sponsor placement

**R19 — Put the ad at the moment the gap is widest, cut it 2× faster than
the story, and verbally bracket both ends.**
> Evidence: the 29s read starts at **2:48** — three seconds after the
> title card, i.e. the instant the war has been declared started and
> before a single frame of it has been shown. It is cut at 12.4 cuts/min
> against the body's 6.4, and bracketed by *"hey, before I dive into this"*
> (2:48) and *"for now, let's dive into this"* (3:17).

## 3. Where this contradicts what we already do

Three of our standing defaults come from the two Hangtime studies. This
video is a genuine counter-example to two of them, and the reconciliation
is not "Hangtime was wrong" — it is that the two channels solve different
problems.

| Our current default | This video | Reconciliation |
|---|---|---|
| **Hook cuts ~2–2.4× faster than the body** (Hangtime: 31.4 vs 12–15 cuts/min) | Hook cuts at **one third** the sponsor rate and *slower* than the body per-minute | Hangtime's hook previews five chapters of *footage*; this hook builds one *graphic*. The rule is not "cut fast" — it is **"change state fast."** Hangtime changes state by cutting; Harris changes state inside a shot. Both hit ~one change every 2–3s. |
| **2.8–3.0s median for connective tissue** | 4.25s whole-video median; 40–61s on graphic shots | We have no third device. Our tissue is footage, so our median stands — but a Ninth Room *explainer* beat, when we build one, should be allowed to run 20–40s on a single evolving graphic instead of being chopped to 3s. |
| **Peak-protected rhythm: the longest shots are the emotional peaks** | Holds, and strengthens: two engineered *silences* (19s, 13s) sit at the peak | **Adopt as-is.** This is the third independent confirmation across three studies and two creators. It should stop being a finding and become a rule. |

Two more places where we and he diverge, and **we win both**:

- He captions **none** of his own narration. `workflows/platform-specs.md`
  requires burned-in captions for mute-first viewing. Ours stands; his
  audience watches on a laptop with sound.
- He writes **no numbers on screen** — every statistic is spoken only. Our
  `stat` card exists precisely so a parent can pause and check it.
  Ours stands.

## 4. For our doctrines

Each rule mapped to the craft it feeds and the artefact it would change.
"Adopt" means it can go into an agent brief as written. "Translate" means
the technique is right but the scale or subject must change. "Reject"
means our brand outranks it.

### Story — `story-designer`, `edit_plan.json`

| Rule | Verdict | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| **R1** title holds the gap, hook holds momentum | **Adopt** | The hook beat may open *in the middle of the day*, on a real moment, without restating the title. It must not open with "today we're at…". The gap lives in the title + thumbnail; the hook's job is velocity. |
| **R17** three tenses | **Translate** | Present tense for the visit ("we're walking into…"), past only for the reveal's resolution, present for the takeaway. Our voice is a family narrating a day, so the historical present is already natural — this just names it. |
| **R18** narrator is not a character | **Reject** | Our ensemble *is* the show (`CLAUDE.md`, "One voice… nobody on camera is 'the host'"). A 0%-face episode would not be a Ninth Room episode. Keep the number as a warning in the other direction only: he proves an explainer stretch can run several minutes with no face, so a long exhibit explanation does **not** need a cutaway to a talking head every 20s. |
| **R19** ad at peak gap | **N/A** | We have no sponsor. Noted for the day we do. |
| **R14** engineered silence at the peak | **Adopt** | `edit_plan.json` should be allowed a beat with **no VO and no caption** — footage and sound only — at the episode's single biggest moment. 10–15s at our length. This is the third study to land on peak protection; it is now a rule, not an observation. |
| **R16** payoff is the opening canvas, stripped | **Adopt** | The last beat should return to the episode's first image with every overlay removed. For us: the establishing wide of the room, held clean, no card, at the takeaway. |

### Coverage — `coverage-editor`, the b-roll grammar

| Rule | Verdict | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| **R10** footage composites onto the spine, doesn't replace it | **Translate** | Our spine is the face and the room, not a map — but the principle is the missing fifth grammar term. Today a cover is `establish / illustrate / foretell / bridge`, all of which *replace* the shot. This video shows a fifth: **overlay** — the cover plays as an inset while the primary shot continues. We have `callout` (a square frame with crop marks) which is exactly this container. |
| **R11** point, don't name | **Adopt** | Directly reinforces `illustrate` ("cut to the thing being NAMED, within about a second of the word"). Add its inverse as a writing note: when the shot shows the thing, the line should say *"right here"*, not the noun. Sofia and Alma already talk this way on camera; the edit should stop fighting it. |
| **R13** fly to the familiar at the same scale | **Translate** | We can't fly a map, but `scale` and `compare` do this job: when a room's fact involves a size or distance, put it next to something the family knows. Only when it is *named*, never as decoration — two uses in 16 minutes is the ceiling, not the floor. |
| **R3** four shots in 72s | **Reject as a target** | His cold open is one graphic. Ours is faces and a room, and the Hangtime hook rhythm still governs. Keep as a licence, not a target: **a hook shot is allowed to run 15s if it is genuinely changing.** |

### Captions — `caption-editor`, `captions.json`

| Rule | Verdict | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| **R15** sourced quote card, two-tier | **Adopt — highest value in the study** | When an episode quotes a plaque, a guide, a sign or an archival voice, the caption should carry **the quote in brand type and a smaller attribution line naming the source**. This is `CLAUDE.md` rule 1 ("Accuracy is the brand… a parent is fact-checking this in front of their kid") given a visual form we do not currently have. |
| His zero-captioning of VO | **Reject** | `workflows/platform-specs.md` requires burned-in captions. Non-negotiable. |
| **R9** name resolves 1.5s after the spoken name | **Adopt** | A name or term card should *start* within half a second of the word and *finish* about a second and a half later. Our `lower_third` already animates; this is the timing target. |

### Cards — `graphics-director`, `graphics_plan.json`, `pipeline/overlay_kit.py`

| Rule | Verdict | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| **R8** chapter marker as a bar, never a break | **Adopt** | We already have this and did not know it was validated: the `chapter` card's door meter is our version of his date strip. This study says **keep it on screen through the turn instead of cutting to it** — and gives the cadence: one section marker every ~34s of story. |
| **R6/R7** persistent badges with state | **Translate — this is the real gap** | We have no persistent-actor graphic. For us the actors are Caleb, Alma and Sofia, and the "state" is who's currently winning a bet, holding a guess, or ahead on the scoreboard. `scoreboard`, `streak` and `vote` are the pieces; nothing keeps them *resident*. This is worth a design brief. |
| **R4** state change every ~3s inside a held graphic | **Adopt** | A card that holds longer than 6 seconds must visibly change at least every 3. Currently our cards animate in and then sit. |
| **R12** counted nouns get counted objects | **Adopt** | When a line says a number under about eight, render that many objects rather than the digit. The `chapter` door meter already does this for chapters; extend the idea. |
| **R5** title card lands on the sting, 3s, after the setup | **Translate** | Our `hook` screen currently opens the episode. This says a title can wait until the setup is done and land on a musical accent. Worth testing on one episode, not adopting blind — our retention profile is not his. |
| His filled black plates, gold + red + white in one frame | **Reject** | Violates **"No filled plates, and one yellow moment per frame"** — enforced in `overlay_kit.py` and non-negotiable. Our date-strip equivalent must be chalk type with a gradient scrim or the double-shadow, one accent, no box. |
| His zero emoji | **Reject** | `brand/voice-and-tone.md`: emoji are encouraged (Caleb, 2026-08-20). |
| His zero on-screen numbers | **Reject** | Our `stat` card is a fact-checking affordance. Keep it. |

### Sound — `sound-designer`, the cue sheet

| Rule | Verdict | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| **R14** the 19s and 13s silences | **Adopt** | These are the two loudest moments in the video and they are cues, not gaps: score down to a single sustained element, no SFX, no stinger. The cue sheet should be able to write **a rest** as an explicit cue. |
| **R5** title lands on the sting | **Adopt** | The title/chapter graphic should be timed to a music accent, not to the spoken phrase. Gives the cue sheet an anchor it currently lacks. |
| **R8** odometer roll on the chapter marker | **Adopt** | A mechanical tick under the door-meter change — small, once per chapter turn. Sits naturally in the existing transition-whoosh vocabulary. |

### Brand non-negotiables that outrank everything above

Stated explicitly so no future agent reads a rule out of this file and
applies it over a doctrine:

1. **Burned-in captions, always** (`workflows/platform-specs.md`). R-nothing
   here licenses dropping them.
2. **No filled plates. One yellow moment per frame** (`CLAUDE.md`,
   enforced in `overlay_kit.py`). His entire graphic language breaks this.
   Ours does not bend.
3. **Emoji are encouraged** (`brand/voice-and-tone.md`, Caleb 2026-08-20).
4. **The family is on camera** — ensemble, no host. A faceless explainer is
   a technique we may borrow *inside* an episode, never as an episode.
5. **Fun first, funny while learning.** This video contains zero jokes in
   16:26. Every craft rule taken from it must survive being funny.
6. **Accuracy is the brand.** R15 is the one rule here that actively
   *serves* a doctrine rather than testing it — adopt it hardest.
7. **The payoff must always land.** R1 works only because his payoff lands
   completely. Borrowing "the title carries the gap" without that is
   bait-and-switch, which we don't do.
8. **Design system first** (`CLAUDE.md` rule 6). Nothing in §4 may be
   implemented as a new screen in `overlay_kit.py` directly — the sourced-
   quote card and the persistent-actor rail must be briefed into Claude
   Design and re-implemented from what comes back.

## The three highest-leverage changes

1. **Add the sourced-quote caption.** A quote card with an attribution
   sub-line, so plaques, guides and signs can be quoted with their source
   visible. It is the only technique in this study that directly executes
   an existing doctrine we currently have no visual for. *(R15, evidence
   10:32 and 10:37.)* Needs a Claude Design brief before any code.

2. **Let a card hold, if it changes.** Today our cards animate in and sit;
   a graphic that holds 6+ seconds should change state at least every 3.
   That single change is what lets a 40-second explainer beat exist
   without dying, and it is the mechanism behind the two longest shots in
   a 10-million-view video. *(R4, evidence 0:21 / 0:24 / 0:27 / 0:30
   inside one unbroken 54.6s shot.)*

3. **Write the rest into the cue sheet.** The story-designer may plan one
   beat with no VO and no caption at the episode's peak, and the
   sound-designer must be able to write silence as an explicit cue rather
   than the absence of one. Third study in a row to land on peak
   protection; this is the version with a number on it. *(R14, evidence
   9:21–9:40 = 19s and 10:25–10:38 = 13s.)*
