# Taste sign-off cards

Prepared 2026-08-28 for Caleb's sign-off sitting on `brand/taste.md` — the 30
statements mined 2026-08-24 from his recorded verdicts. That file's header
reads **"UNSIGNED DRAFT — no doctrine may cite this until Caleb signs off
statement by statement."** This document is the sitting.

## How the sitting runs

- **Statement by statement, binary first.** Each card is read aloud or
  skimmed, and the answer is **keep** or **reject**. "Revise" exists only
  when Caleb volunteers different wording on the spot — nobody drafts
  alternatives for him during the sitting.
- **Budget: about 30 minutes.** Most cards are a 30–45 second call. Two are
  not: **T15** and **T28** carry real contradictions and are budgeted 3–4
  minutes each. If time runs short, they are worth doing first.
- **What happens to each answer.** A verdict line is written into
  `brand/taste.md` directly under the statement:
  - `verdict: kept 2026-MM-DD`
  - `verdict: revised 2026-MM-DD` — his new wording replaces the heading
  - `verdict: rejected 2026-MM-DD — <his reason>`

  Rejected statements **stay in the file with their evidence**. They are
  mined data; deleting them destroys the trace back to the verdicts. The
  ratchet simply makes them uncitable — no agent brief may reference a
  rejected statement.
- **Unfinished is fine.** A statement without a verdict stays unsigned and
  uncitable, exactly as today. A short sitting that signs ten statements
  cleanly is a better outcome than thirty rushed calls.

## The one caveat that applies everywhere

Every cutting and card statement below rests on a **single episode** — the
HMNS review. The cruise episode has not been through a Caleb review round, so
nothing here has been confirmed on a second subject. This is stated once,
here, rather than on all 30 cards. Six statements are thinner still and carry
their own warning.

## Cutting: where a beat starts and stops

### T1 — "A beat may not end before the last spoken word finishes"
**In plain words:** When the end of a clip cuts off a word, the fix is always to let the sentence finish — never to trim tighter.

**If you keep it:** story-designer.md and shot-fixer.md cite it — tail trims default to extending through the final word, and a fixer resolving a clipped tail may only extend, never shave.
**If you reject it:** the validator's never-cut-mid-sentence rule stays; extend-over-trim stays an uncited habit.
